01

Where the shock landed

3 sea and land routes out of the Gulf broke at once, and the price of money moved.

The Strait of Hormuz is a channel between Iran and Oman about 3 kilometres wide at its narrowest, and before the war it carried more than 20 million barrels of oil a day. Industry estimates now put the flow at 6 to 9 million. The Bab el-Mandeb Strait at the far end of the Red Sea is the alternative, and Houthi forces have seized Mayyun Island inside it and the port of Mocha beside it. The third way out is overland: Saudi Aramco's East–West pipeline carries crude across the country to the Red Sea so tankers never enter Hormuz, and Saudi Arabia shut it after drone attacks from Iraq set it alight. All 3 routes are damaged at once, which is why crude, freight and fuel repriced together instead of one after another.

Brent crude came within reach of $110 a barrel, Shanghai crude hit $121 and the gap between them widened to a record $16, which means Chinese buyers are paying well above the global benchmark for physical cargo. The sharpest move is in diesel, the fuel that runs freight, farm equipment and standby generators: the average US retail price passed $6 a gallon for the first time and reached $6.20, up 78% in 9 months.

Diesel sits inside the cost of almost every physical good, so a diesel shock raises measured inflation, which forces central banks to raise rates, which raises the cost of money for everyone planning to borrow. The 10-year US Treasury yield — the rate the American government pays to borrow for 10 years, and the reference price for most long-term debt in the world — touched 5% for the first time since 2023. A shipping problem in a 3-kilometre channel is now setting the discount rate on every long-duration asset.

02

The shortages money cannot fix quickly

Evening power, gas turbines, coal stock and purification lines — constraints with waiting lists, not price tags.

India's electricity demand rose 12.8% in a month to about 169 billion units and peak demand about 12% to 258 gigawatts. The revealing part is the price split. During solar hours the average market clearing price was ₹1.62 a unit against ₹1.61 a year earlier, effectively unchanged. Outside solar hours it rose about 33% to ₹5.3. India has solved daytime electricity and not evening electricity, so every new air conditioner and every new data centre bids for the same thin supply after sunset.

The reserve behind that supply is thinner than the output figures suggest. Coal-fired generation rose 13% and coal's share went to 63% from 62%, but coal stocks at thermal plants fell to 29 million tonnes from 50 million a year earlier. Renewable output rose about 27% on 16.3 gigawatts of new capacity, which lifts the daytime figure and leaves the evening one alone.

The data centre build has the same shape, with the constraint sitting in equipment and people rather than money. Gas turbines are short, so developers are going back to boilers and steam, and factory-built modular power units are sold out because they cut delivery timelines by up to 40% and on-site labour by as much as 70%. Moody's puts the American data centre power buildout at $110 billion for 45 gigawatts through 2030, over 30 gigawatts of it gas. October is the last month to start building anything meant to run before the end of 2027.

03

The decimal that moved the Fed

Not oil. Memory chips, phone bills, and a 10 basis point push into a rate rise.

American consumer prices rose 0.4% in a month and 3.4% over a year, both as expected. Core inflation — the same basket with food and energy removed, because those move for reasons a central bank cannot control — rose 0.3% against an expected 0.2%. That decimal was the difference between a hold and a rise, and within hours traders priced about a 90% chance of a rise, then 2 rises by year end.

It did not come from fuel. Gasoline rose 3.9% and made up over a third of the headline increase, but gasoline is excluded from core. What pushed core over the line was a record jump in cell phone service prices, driven by the cost of memory chips, which added about 10 basis points — a basis point is a hundredth of 1% — and Goldman Sachs confirmed roughly that contribution afterwards. So the artificial intelligence build consumes memory, memory raises the price of phone plans, phone plans sit inside core inflation, and core inflation is what the Federal Reserve reacts to. The AI trade raised the cost of money for everybody who is not in it.

The repricing was not only American: traders now fully price 4 quarter-point rises from the European Central Bank and 5 from the Bank of England by the end of 2027. Households are reading it as inflation rather than tightening, with sentiment down to 47.8 against an expected 51 and inflation expected over the coming year up to 4.6%. Household net worth rose $12.5 trillion in a quarter to a record $185.7 trillion, because the assets are held by people who already own them: the income needed to afford an average American home is a record $124,674 against a median household income of $86,159.

Capacity is not flow

The oil number everyone is quoting is the size of the pipe, not what was going through it.

The figure attached to the Saudi pipeline almost everywhere is 7 million barrels a day. That is its maximum capacity. Flows ran at roughly 4 to 5 million a day through the first 5 months of the war and had already fallen to about 2 million by August. So the shutdown removes about 2 million barrels a day of actual movement, not 7 and not 4. Reported estimates put the closure at 4% to 5% of world supply, anchored much closer to the capacity figure than the flow figure. Any version of the claim can be checked with 1 question: is the number capacity or throughput?

Hormuz gets the same treatment, usually described as running at about a fifth of its pre-war level. Against a pre-war figure above 20 million barrels a day and current estimates of 6 to 9 million, it is closer to a third. The International Energy Agency expects world oil supply to fall about 5.7 million barrels a day this year, roughly 6% of global supply, and that is the number that frames the shortage rather than any single pipeline.

Demand is moving the other way and gets far less attention. Sinopec expects Chinese oil demand to fall 8.9% this year, and although China's crude imports rose for a 2nd month they are still 23% below a year earlier with stockpiles above 1.2 billion barrels. The largest buyer can stand aside for months, which is why a supply shock this size has not produced a larger price. Food moved with fuel because diesel moves food: the Bloomberg Agriculture Spot Index rose over 13% in a month, its largest monthly gain since July 2012, with sugar up 11.9% and wheat up 15% over 12 months.

India: a record reserve and a falling rupee

The dollars arrived through a window that has since closed, and the currency fell anyway.

India's foreign exchange reserves rose $44.9 billion in a single week to a record $785.71 billion, the largest weekly gain ever recorded, taking India past Russia to 4th largest holder and lifting reserves above the country's external debt for the first time in 23 months. Almost all of it came from 1 source. FCNR(B) is a window that lets Indians abroad park foreign currency with Indian banks at a fixed rate for a fixed term, and it raised $127.2 billion by the time it closed, far past expectations of $90 billion to $100 billion, with nearly half of it 5-year money. The Reserve Bank of India is absorbing the entire hedging cost, so the currency risk sits on the central bank's balance sheet rather than the banks'.

In the same days the rupee had its largest weekly fall against the dollar in 4 months and the 10-year government bond yield touched 7%. Retail inflation rose to 4.82% with food at 5.95%, a 3rd consecutive month above the 4% target, and the Reserve Bank announced ₹1 lakh crore of bond sales to drain liquidity, which is the central bank taking back the rupees those dollars created. The reserve is a stock built by a window that has shut; the flows are still going the other way. Foreign investors pulled ₹13,138 crore out of Indian equities in the month so far, are heading for a 3rd straight year of net outflow, and foreign ownership of listed Indian companies is at a 17-year low.

The trade opening is the other half of policy, and it is slower than the headline suggests. The European Commission has sent the India–EU agreement to the Council with signature targeted by year end, and almost every concession on both sides is capped by volume and phased over 5 to 10 years.

India: assembly is a tenth of the chain

A policy that worked exactly as designed, and a trade deficit that grew because of it.

99.2% of the phones sold in India are assembled in India. Assembly captures 8% to 10% of the value of an electronic product. The other 80% to 90% — components, printed circuit boards, chips — still comes from China, Taiwan, Japan and South Korea. That pair of numbers explains how a policy success and a widening deficit are the same event described twice.

A study by Koan Advisory Group and the Institute of Chinese Studies counted it line by line. China supplied at least 80% of India's imports across 71 electronics product lines, up from 44 in 2018-19, and 46 of those 71 crossed the threshold only after 2018-19, so the dependence deepened while the assembly plants were being built. The exposure is in the parts feeding Indian lines, so restricting finished imports does nothing to it. Cost is why it persists: Nokia's India head puts the price gap on locally sourced components at 20% to 80%, and Lupin's finance chief puts Indian logistics costs 14% to 15% above China's with dearer electricity.

The companies climbing out describe an identical method. Amber Enterprises started with sheet metal in air conditioners 25 years ago, added 1 component every 3 years, and now makes 70% of the bill of materials in-house; it is repeating the sequence in electronics on a 5 to 6 year timeline, which makes its assembly deal with Oppo the entry ticket rather than the business. Sona Comstar is doing the same in robotics, and its 3 margin bands say where the value is: components at 25% to 30%, perception and engineering at 35% to 45%, complete platforms at 15% to 22%. The best margin sits in the middle of the chain, not at either end.

Aerospace shows how far up an Indian supplier can already get: Aequs won a 15-year single-source contract for fully assembled Airbus A320 wheels, work Safran had never moved outside its own plants.

India: revenue rising, volume falling

In jewellery the price is doing the work; in beer and beauty the customer is.

GRT Jewellers is buying control of Tribhovandas Bhimji Zaveri for up to ₹1,033.71 crore. On reported figures the target looks excellent: revenue from ₹1,342 crore to ₹3,203 crore over 5 years and net profit from ₹44 crore to ₹203 crore, with margin moving from 6.6% to 11.3%. The volumes say something else. Gold sold fell from 3,424 kg to 2,249 kg over 3 years and diamonds from 47,371 carats to 39,031. The revenue growth is the gold price, and the market had already worked that out, marking the company down from ₹1,223 crore to ₹744 crore while profit tripled.

Where the customer is genuinely trading up the numbers look different. Premium beer went from 12% of Indian beer sales in 2021 to 22% now, with premium volumes growing about 30% a year against a beer market that compounded 12% over 5 years while spirits managed 5%. Some of that is tax design rather than taste: Karnataka moved to duty linked to alcohol content, so weaker beer now carries a lighter tax than stronger beer, reversing the incentive that made Indian beer strong and cheap. AB InBev expects premium and super-premium brands at about 75% of its India business within 3 to 4 years, from 60%.

Beauty is the same shift with a takeover market attached. The market is heading for $40 billion by 2030, online is growing 35% a year against 14% to 18% for the category, and gross margins run 65% to 75%, which is why more than ₹17,000 crore has gone into buying Indian beauty brands in 3 years, by FSN E-Commerce Ventures, L'Oréal, Hindustan Unilever and others. The mechanism is arithmetic: a direct-to-consumer brand hits a digital ceiling somewhere between ₹150 crore and ₹500 crore of revenue, and getting past it needs offline distribution it cannot fund.

The ceiling at the top of the market is visible in 1 product. Apple's 256GB Pro model is ₹1,64,900 in India against ₹1,14,576 in the United States, which is about 2.2% of annual income per person in America and about 112% in India, and it is expected to post its first annual decline in Indian unit sales.

Where the price stopped reading the filing

2 disclosures with specific, checkable problems, and 2 reminders that growth is not a return.

ESDS Software has doubled since listing and trades at 168 times earnings, with roughly 95 lakh shares changing hands daily against a free float of 1.4 crore, so about 67% of the tradable stock turns over every day. The filings raise 5 separate problems. The headline “$1.25 billion deal” is money the company must pay out, not revenue it will collect. A ₹1,177 crore advance from 1 unnamed customer equals 251% of revenue and about 98% of operating cash flow, and it is refundable. The supplier of the graphics processors is a small company that has itself been the target of a short-seller attack. 45% of group profit sits in a subsidiary the main auditor never audited, and the promoter personally owns 1% of that subsidiary.

Coforge's problem has a precise finding attached. Chairman O P Bhatt resigned after the internal auditor, KPMG, found the board evaluation report had been presented without copies circulated to other directors, and that the chairman's own category had received the lowest rating in that report — a finding not disclosed to the board. The committee chair resigned days later citing tension between independent and executive directors, which the company called unfounded. Coforge says none of this bears on financial performance, which may be exactly true, but a premium multiple on a mid-tier services company is partly a price paid for governance, so the multiple is what is at risk rather than the earnings.

The reverse case is worth as much. Bajaj Finance compounded profit at 34% a year over 5 years while its share price compounded at 7%, because price-to-book fell from about 12 times to 5.6. Lupin was worth about ₹95,000 crore in 2015 and about the same 11 years later, and at 5% to 6% inflation a 2015 rupee buys about half what it did. Growth was delivered in both. The rating was not kept, and the rating is most of the return. Meanwhile about ₹24,500 crore of new issues arrives in a single truncated week, into a market where only 38 of 377 Indian listings between 2005 and 2015 beat the benchmark over the following 3 years.

A speed limit only 1 side is discussing

The argument about slowing artificial intelligence, and the half-trillion dollars of contracts signed while it ran.

Anthropic's chief executive Dario Amodei published an essay arguing that frontier development should be paced, and committed the company to giving outside evaluators permanent, employee-level access to its systems. OpenAI's Sam Altman and Elon Musk agreed in public. Within 2 days President Trump rejected it outright, called it a conspiracy against AI and data centres and said the only beneficiary would be China. Beijing called the proposal a cold war playbook, and Justin Lin, an architect of Alibaba's Qwen models, wrote that nobody needs anyone's permission.

The capital did not pause while the argument ran, which is worth more than the essays. Nvidia is in talks to put up to $10 billion into Anthropic's offering, part of a raise of as much as $100 billion at a valuation near $2 trillion. Anthropic is profitable for a 2nd straight quarter and its own compute commitments could reach $517 billion, nearly 3 times an earlier estimate. Nvidia, Palantir and Booz Allen Hamilton moved to limit their use of Anthropic's models, and SoftBank fell 13%.

Oracle showed how the money flows. Quarterly capital spending was $28.5 billion but the net cash outlay about $18 billion, the difference covered by customers who pay for the chips up front or bring their own. Processors coming up for renewal, most 4 years or older, were renewed at a 20% premium, and remaining obligations rose to $664 billion. The shares still surrendered a gain of as much as 8.5% and the cost of insuring its debt widened, because the market is pricing the balance sheet rather than the backlog.

China is not in the argument. Domestic compute is expected to reach 30% of the global total by 2030 from 21%. When DeepSeek said it had cut the high-bandwidth memory its latest model needs, SK Hynix fell 2.2% and Samsung Electronics 3.5%. A software efficiency gain in China is a demand cut for Korean hardware, and a live risk to the same memory price that just moved American core inflation. Europe is planning for permanently less: Volkswagen is building a cost base that breaks even at around 8 million cars and will close a plant if no other solution is found.

Arguments worth keeping open

5 disagreements where the 2 sides are using different numbers, and how a reader can settle each one.

The richest of them is about leverage. The standard list of Indian answers to Chinese pressure is a trade deal with Taiwan, arming Beijing's rivals in the South China Sea, sailing the navy through, raising the Dalai Lama's profile. Each costs China pride and none costs it anything it needs. The cards on the other side are different in kind: China froze rare-earth magnet shipments early in 2025 and Maruti Suzuki, Tata Motors and Hero MotoCorp were weeks from stopping lines, and it timed fertiliser curbs to the sowing season. More than 70% of the active ingredients India's drug industry runs on come from China, so retaliation on trade shuts Indian factories before it troubles Chinese exporters. The counter-argument is that this is a description of today rather than of the direction, and the 71 product lines counted in section 06 are the way to test it: if that number falls over the next few years, the leverage is closing; if it keeps rising, nothing else in the debate matters.

Words explained

Every term used above, in 1 sentence each.

Strait of HormuzThe sea channel between Iran and Oman, about 3 kilometres wide at its narrowest, through which most Gulf oil leaves by ship.
Bab el-MandebThe narrow entrance to the Red Sea beside Yemen, the exit route for anything that avoids Hormuz by going overland to the west coast.
Brent crudeThe North Sea oil grade used as the global reference price.
Shanghai crudeChina's own oil futures contract; when it trades far above Brent, Chinese buyers are paying up for physical cargo.
Capacity and throughputCapacity is the most a pipeline could carry; throughput is what it was actually carrying. Confusing the 2 is the main error in the current oil coverage.
DieselThe fuel used by trucks, trains, ships, farm equipment and standby generators, so its price passes into almost every physical good.
International Energy AgencyThe Paris-based body that publishes the standard estimates of world oil supply and demand.
Headline inflationThe change in the full basket of consumer prices, including food and fuel.
Core inflationThe same basket with food and energy removed, because those move for reasons a central bank cannot influence; it is the number policy actually reacts to.
Basis pointA hundredth of 1%, so 25 basis points is a quarter of a percentage point.
Federal ReserveThe central bank of the United States, which sets the short-term interest rate that anchors most global borrowing costs.
10-year Treasury yieldThe interest rate the American government pays to borrow for 10 years; it sets the reference price for long-term debt worldwide.
Household net worthEverything American households own minus everything they owe; it rises with asset prices rather than with wages.
Foreign exchange reservesThe stock of foreign currency a central bank holds, used to steady its own currency and to pay for imports in a crisis.
External debtEverything a country owes to lenders outside it, whether government, banks or companies.
FCNR(B)A deposit that lets Indians living abroad park foreign currency with an Indian bank at a fixed rate for a fixed term, repaid in the same currency.
Hedging costWhat it costs to insure against a currency moving against you; here the central bank is paying it instead of the banks.
Open market operationsA central bank buying or selling government bonds to add or remove cash from the banking system.
Foreign portfolio investorAn overseas fund that buys listed shares and bonds without taking operating control.
Free trade agreementA treaty that lowers tariffs and other barriers between 2 economies, usually in stages over years.
Tariff-rate quotaA set volume that may enter at a low duty, with a much higher duty on anything above it.
Bill of materialsThe full list of parts that go into a product; making more of it in-house is how a assembler becomes a manufacturer.
FabA semiconductor fabrication plant, where silicon wafers are turned into chips.
Silicon carbideA compound semiconductor that handles higher voltages and temperatures than plain silicon, used in electric vehicles and power equipment.
High-bandwidth memoryThe stacked memory that sits beside an AI chip; its price is currently moving consumer inflation.
Graphics processorThe chip that does most AI computation, bought or rented in large clusters.
Frontier modelThe most capable AI model a laboratory has built, as opposed to the smaller ones it sells widely.
GigawattA billion watts of electrical capacity, roughly 1 large nuclear reactor or enough for about 750,000 homes.
Billion unitsThe Indian measure of electricity consumed, where 1 unit is 1 kilowatt-hour.
Market clearing priceThe price at which electricity supply and demand match on an exchange for a given time block.
Remaining performance obligationsContracted revenue a company has signed but not yet delivered; a backlog measure, not cash.
Credit default swapA contract that pays out if a borrower defaults; its price is the market's read on how risky the debt is.
BliskA single piece combining a jet engine's disc and its blades, one of the harder things in aerospace to machine.
Single sourceA part only 1 supplier is qualified to make for that customer, which is why it is both valuable and slow to win.
Active pharmaceutical ingredientThe molecule in a medicine that does the work; India makes finished drugs but imports most of these from China.
PeptideA short chain of amino acids; the class of molecule that includes the weight-loss and diabetes drugs.
PurificationThe step that separates the wanted peptide from everything else made alongside it, and the hardest part of the process to copy.
Rare-earth magnetA high-strength magnet needed in motors and speakers; China controls most of the supply and has restricted it before.
EBITDAEarnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation — a rough measure of operating profit before financing and accounting choices.
Operating marginOperating profit as a share of revenue; it shows whether growth is being bought or earned.
Price-to-bookShare price divided by the accounting value of the company's own capital; it can fall while profit rises, which is how a good business gives a poor return.
Free floatThe shares actually available to trade, excluding promoter and locked holdings; a small float makes prices move violently in both directions.
Open offerA required offer to buy shares from public holders when someone takes control of a listed Indian company.
Unlisted marketPrivate trading in shares before a listing, where prices often sit far above the eventual issue price.
Systematic investment planA standing monthly instruction to buy into a mutual fund, which is why Indian retail flows are steadier than the index.
Micro, small, mid and large capSize bands for listed companies by market value; the smaller bands pay more over a decade and fall much harder in a bad year.
DrawdownThe fall from a peak to a trough; the number that decides whether an investor stays in long enough to earn the decade figure.
